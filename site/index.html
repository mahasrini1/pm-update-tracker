Which products are listed. A CRP is listed when a generic submission names (or a predecessor such as Astra Pharma or Zeneca Pharma, allowing for typos) as the CRP company, or when holds the CRP's own NOCs. Generic filers often misspell the CRP name, so one product can appear under several spellings.

Generic status uses the current Drug Product Database (DPD) status of the DINs in the generic NOCs that name the CRP. Generic on market: at least one generic DIN is marketed. Generic approved, not marketed: none is marketed, but at least one is approved or dormant. No active generic: every generic DIN is cancelled or not in the DPD.

CRP market status also comes from the DPD. A CRP is active when at least one of its DINs is currently marketed. Products are matched by DIN first, then by brand name, then by close spelling to catch typos in the CRP names filed with generic submissions. Where a name matches several companies, the CRP company's own products are used. CRPs with no match are listed under "Not found in Drug Product Database". Inactive NOC records are excluded throughout.

Limits. The NOC database lists approved submissions only, so filings still under review do not appear. CRP NOCs are matched by brand name on New Drug Submissions and their supplements. Products approved before 1994 often have no NOC record, and their details are taken from the generic NOCs that reference them.
